Indoor Games for Dogs: Easy Enrichment on Rainy Days

By HappyPupGuide — practical, info-only dog care guides.

Rainy days do not have to mean a bored dog. Dogs need mental work just as much as walks, and ten minutes of a good indoor game can tire a dog out more than a slow half-hour stroll. The games below need no special equipment — just things you already have at home — and they suit puppies, adult dogs, and seniors alike.

1. The muffin tin puzzle

Put a few treats in the cups of a muffin tin and cover each one with a tennis ball. Your dog has to work out how to nose the balls away to reach the food. It is simple, safe, and endlessly repeatable. Start with only two or three covered cups so your dog learns the idea, then cover them all.

2. Find it

Hold a treat where your dog can see it, say "find it", and toss it a short distance. Repeat, then start hiding treats around one room while your dog waits. This game uses the nose, which is a dog's strongest sense, and a few rounds of searching are genuinely tiring. It is also a great way to build a reliable recall word without any pressure.

3. The cardboard box dig

Put treats inside a cardboard box with some scrunched paper or old towels, and let your dog dig them out. Dogs that love to dig get an outlet, and shy dogs gain confidence. Supervise the first few times and take the box away if your dog starts eating the cardboard.

4. Name that toy

Dogs can learn the names of their toys. Pick one toy, say its name every time you play with it, then ask your dog to fetch it by name from a small pile. It sounds like a trick, but it is real brain work — and it is the first step towards a dog that can tidy its own toys away.

5. Stairway or hallway recalls

If you have a hallway, stand at one end and call your dog for a treat, then have a family member call from the other end. Short, happy recalls back and forth are exercise, training, and bonding in one. Keep it short and cheerful, and always end while your dog still wants more.

Keep sessions short

Five to ten minutes per game is enough. Stop while your dog is still enjoying it, and rotate the games across the week so they stay fresh.
